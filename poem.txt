The Key Drawer

My grandmother kept a drawer of keys
to doors that no longer stood:
the flat on Kramat, the shop, a Fiat sold in '71.
I asked her why. She said a key
is the shape of a yes you once could say.

Agency is smaller than they tell you.
Not the river, but the one stone
that decides which way the river bends.
Not the storm, but the hand
that shuts the window, then opens it
so the rain can't say it wasn't asked.

I have been the weather's alibi.
I have said *it happened*
when I meant *I let it*.
Tonight I hold one key against my palm
until it warms to the heat of a choice,

and turn it,
not because a door is waiting,
but because I am the one who turns.
